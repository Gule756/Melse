# MELSE — EXACT UI/UX MASTER PROMPT

You are redesigning the entire UI/UX of **Melse**.

IMPORTANT:

The previous implementation became too complicated and lost the original product idea.

**Do not build another generic marketplace, admin dashboard, or SaaS template.**

Melse is a **service-on-demand application**.

The customer's mental model is:

> **“Something is broken. I need someone trustworthy to fix it.”**

The UI must make that experience extremely fast.

The application should feel like a combination of:

* Uber-level simplicity
* modern service marketplace
* trusted local technician network
* Ethiopian-first service platform

But Melse must have its own identity.

---

# 1. THE MOST IMPORTANT UI RULE

When a customer opens Melse, they should NOT see:

* complicated dashboards
* statistics
* huge amounts of text
* dozens of categories
* complicated menus
* unnecessary onboarding
* giant forms
* confusing filters

They should immediately see:

> **What do you need help with?**

Then the services.

The entire application should revolve around this.

# 49. VISUAL DESIGN

The visual identity should communicate:

**Trust + speed + professionalism + local accessibility**

Use:

* Clean typography
* Strong hierarchy
* Large touch targets
* Moderate rounded corners
* Subtle shadows
* High-quality icons
* Plenty of whitespace
* Clear status indicators

Avoid:

* Neon colors
* Excessive gradients
* Glassmorphism everywhere
* Huge animations
* Gaming-style UI
* Excessive decorative illustrations
* Generic AI-generated dashboard aesthetics

---

# 60. FINAL DESIGN TEST

Before finishing, pretend you are a customer who has never seen Melse before.

Give yourself this problem:

> “My kitchen sink is leaking.”

Open the app.

Ask:

1. Do I immediately know what Melse does?
2. Can I find Plumbing immediately?
3. Can I explain my problem easily?
4. Can I provide my location?
5. Can I understand the expected price?
6. Can I see trustworthy technicians?
7. Can I understand when someone will arrive?
8. Can I book without confusion?
9. Can I track the technician?
10. Can I pay easily?
11. Do I know that my service is guaranteed?
12. Can I find the history later?

If the answer to any is NO:

**Fix the UI.**

Do not add another feature.

---

# FINAL INSTRUCTION TO THE AI BUILDER

The goal is NOT to make the application look impressive to a developer.

The goal is to make a person with a broken pipe, broken refrigerator, electrical problem, or dirty home think:

# “Oh. I can get someone through this.”

And then:

# “I want to use this.”

Build the UI around that reaction.

**Simple for customers.**

**Fast for technicians.**

**Powerful for administrators.**

**Scalable underneath.**

Do not allow technical architecture to dictate the user experience.

The user experience comes first.
